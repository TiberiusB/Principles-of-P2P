Materials are long Google Docs with theory first, highlighted text for skimming, signalization boxes, and (unevenly) exercises or mapping sessions. The public-facing list on [sensorica.co](https://www.sensorica.co/stewarding/help/education/collaborative-entrepreneurship) originally named **11 parts**; only **eight** documents exist in Program Manual, and two of those are stubs. Numbering of Parts 6 and 7 disagrees between the website, the structure slides, and the Drive files.

## Summary of each Part



### Part 1 — What’s possible?

**Intent.** Open the field of possibles. Assess what participants already think “collaborative” means. Fix vocabulary. Situate the learner in a historical transition (on the order of feudalism → capitalism), then ask them to choose a posture: traditional firm, extreme p2p, or hybrid.

**What it actually contains.**

- A reclaiming of the term: “collaborative entrepreneur” in business-school English often means internal synergy or social entrepreneurship. Sensorica uses it for the **collaborative economy** in the CBPP sense.
- **Myths to unlearn:** nothing without money; scarcity as worldview; competitiveness as virtue; value only for customers; specialists in tight teams; no contracts, no integrity.
- **Lead case — Matrioshka:** open furniture prototype after Eco2Fest / Ouishare / Quatorze, continued at the Sensorica lab. Logged cost about $77,000; cash about $2,000; under two months. Social contract: log contributions, share future benefits; some people opted out of logging for learning and exposure. The point is not “cheap labor” but **cocreation and shared risk** instead of wage-mediated cooperation.
- Stubs or thin cases: Precious Plastics, Farm Hack (useful quote on entrepreneurial coalitions around a commons), Guerilla Translation.
- **Context:** ICT as transformative; Linux/Wikipedia as anomalies for classical economics; Benkler / *Coase’s Penguin*; hybrid firms (Red Hat, Adafruit) vs OVNs (Bitcoin, Sensorica).
- Dated technology panorama (IoT/blockchain forecasts from ~2016–2017) and an unfinished “strategic intelligence applied to p2p.” Todo: also talk about Holochain and develop on the consequences of AI, also AI plus p2p. 
- **Three economic patterns:** platform capitalism, platform coop, p2p (nondominium platforms, meritocratic governance). Comparison table.
- **Collaborative vs sharing economy:** sharing platforms often mediate exchange; collaborative economy is co-production of commons plus benefit-return.
- **Types of entrepreneurs** and a first skill list (crowd processes, attractors, synergy, socialize work).
- **Key concepts** (network mindset, transparency, openness, stigmergy, synergy, emergence, scarcity/abundance, competition vs collaboration, team/community/network). Several of these are still “to be continued.” ToDo: propose content in relation to "to be continued"
- **The collaborative enterprise:** makerspaces, agile/open-source methods, NRP vs ERP, meritocratic governance, emergent roles, Custodian and Exchange firm. Characteristic list (openness, transparency, risk, ownership, internal relations) that matches the OVN [Venture](https://ovn.world/index.php?title=Venture) page. ToDo: see if we can improve this section from [https://ovn.world/index.php?title=Venture](https://ovn.world/index.php?title=Venture) and from the local what-is-collaboartive-entrepreneurship.md
- Participant suggestions (Sociocracy, Theory U, mutual credit) and contributor credits (Tibi, Wael, Jaime, Antonio).

**Pedagogical form.** Lecture-essay with one strong story, then a long conceptual landscape. The student is asked to *locate themselves*, not yet to *do* a venture. The closing claim of the introduction is that an entrepreneur should think first about **organizational architecture**, then planning, then stewarding — but Part 1 itself is almost entirely architecture-as-worldview. ToDo: resolve this issue of incompletness: Part 1 itself is almost entirely architecture-as-worldview.  

**Completion.** Usable as a manifesto-session if trimmed. Incomplete as a taught module: unfinished cases, unfinished concept entries, no timebox, no facilitation script, no check that the learner can *explain* an open venture in their own words. ToDo: make a plan and propose how to move to Completion.

### Part 2 — Map the value system

**Intent (from the structure slides).** Define seed value; build a visual of the value system; trace pathways; draw insights; name problems.

**What it actually contains.**

- Organizations as **complex dynamical systems**; mapping **flows** rather than organigrams.
- Edge of the collaborative entrepreneur: **communities and intangible assets** (Allee: informal exchanges create trust and innovation pathways).
- Competing definitions: generic value network; Allee; Sensorica (agent-driven system that provides a **value experience**; living/autopoietic). Sensorica’s definition of value: **what moves human beings into action** (subjective experience). ToDo: put these definitions in historical perspective, Sensorica's definition must be seen as a precoursor, a more evolved version. 
- Building blocks: resources/resource types, agents, processes (eight process types: create, transform, consume/use, destroy, store, infuse, exchange/transfer, share), roles vs positions, activities vs processes.
- **Current-see** (Metacurrency): symbolic systems that structure flows; money is one current-see; entrepreneurs *design* current-sees.
- Plural valuables: market/exchange, use/commons/regenerative, social, ecological, care; second-order revenue (Google: use-value → exchange-value). ToDo: mention distributed economic models patterns, since these patterns define the value system. 
- Resource flows in an OVN: open enterprise not bounded by internal stock; crowdsourcing and affiliation; donations, exchanges, contributions; value streams feeding the benefit-redistribution algorithm.
- **Seed value:** initial attractor (gadget, social good, or bundle of intangibles); genesis concept for an OVN.
- **Mapping session:** collaborative canvas (Metamaps suggested); seed → resources (material, digital, intangible) → processes → agents → roles → relationships/deliverables → main flows → insights → problems → network development questions → design current-sees. ToDo: analyze this with information from our external documents, OVN wiki, P2P Foundatino, etc., and propose improvements.   

**Pedagogical form.** This is the first Part that looks like a **workshop**. Theory is still long; the session steps are real but under-specified (no example map, no time per step, no “good enough” criterion, NRP-CAS recommended without a sandbox).

**Completion.** Strong conceptual core. Needs a worked example (Matrioshka or a student’s venture) and a facilitator’s timing sheet. ToDo: make a plan and propose how to move to Completion.

### Part 3 — Vision, Mission, Positioning

**Intent.** Write vision, mission, positioning; keep them coherent with the value-system map.

**What it actually contains.**

- Collaborative entrepreneur as **movement-maker**; V/M/P as **manifesto**, not only branding. ToDo: analyse and provide feedback, is this adequate, can it be improved? 
- Blurred org/market boundary: clients can become contributors; the “market” is both sink of offerings and source of talent/resources. ToDo: perhaps connect to the value system and economic models patterns. 
- Vision: inspirational, long-horizon, filter and attractor, activates seed value. Algorithm: objective / advantage / scope.
- Mission: motivational, present-tense, alignment/culture/action; moonshot vs incremental; fractal expansion of the venture into an uncontrollable periphery — mission as cohesion and filter. SMART objectives. Examples mix Sensorica/Metamaps with Amazon, Apple, Google.
- Positioning: starts as classical marketing (distinctive strength, niche, price, competitors, crossing the chasm). Then a **ToDo**: less competition, more ecosystem role, collaborative tone. Duplicate notes from Wael (Albaydar session) pasted twice.
- Short “relations to value system / seed value” prompts without a method.
- Sources of information: empty placeholders. ToDo: propose fix.

**Pedagogical form.** Classic strategy-class handout (purpose, key elements, algorithm, corporate examples). The Sensorica-specific claim (manifesto for a field that radiates) is the valuable part; the positioning section currently teaches **startup competitive positioning** and then apologizes for it (ToDo: propose how to fix that).

**Completion.** Half-finished. No student template, no peer critique protocol, no test of whether the manifesto would actually attract contributors (as opposed to pleasing a teacher). ToDo: make a plan and propose how to move to Completion.

### Part 4 — Resources

**Intent.** Optimize the flow of resources into processes; understand intangible assets and property regimes.

**What it actually contains.**

- REA-aligned definition: resources as things agents create, use, consume, destroy, exchange; inputs/outputs of processes. ToDo: align with Valueflows (see your own indexed docs for that. )
- Contrast with the firm: private ownership lowers allocation negotiation; scarcity box (headcount, budget); ownership as power (owners vs workers). Collaborative ventures: sharing/crowdsourcing → relative **abundance of access**; commons, pools of shareables, nondominium to reduce renegotiation.
- Consequences of openness, transparency, decentralization, horizontalism for resource flow (equipotentiality, holoptism, heterarchy). Private property as possible **attack vector** (Uber-like platform capture).
- Taxonomies: value-chain stage (idea → design → study → prototype → product); production inputs (labor, tools, method, space, currency); accessibility; availability (abundant/scarce, rival/non-rival); property regime; transferability; scope (project / network / public); source; behavior (material/immaterial, tangible/intangible — long intangible list: brand, trust, culture, governance…).
- Physical space as bundle of access rights; infrastructure-building must be incentivized; warning against infrastructure investors claiming a cut of *all* future projects; proposed market-equivalent + risk premium payback.
- **NRP vs ERP:** helicopter/network view vs one-firm chain view; resource types, recipes, lists. ToDo: extend to the nondominium hApp [https://github.com/Sensorica/nondominium](https://github.com/Sensorica/nondominium) 
- Skills as properties of agents; types of work treated as resources in NRP.
- **Funds:** no central budget vs custodian + virtual tokens vs forward-looking CAS; fundraising as a deliverable that must belong to the community if the brand/commons were used. ToDo: also include a list of new funding mechanisms built on web3 / blockchqain, as well as possibilitiwes built on Unyt [https://unyt.co/](https://unyt.co/) 

**Pedagogical form.** Ontology lecture. Excellent as a reference chapter; weak as a session. A learner cannot “optimize flows” from this text without a live inventory and a property-regime map of *their* venture. ToDo: propose how to fix that. 

**Completion.** Content-rich, pedagogy-thin. Jump link in the header wrongly titles Part 5 as “Innovation and Feedback.” ToDo: make a plan and propose how to move to Completion.

### Part 5 — Information and Feedback

**Intent (slides).** Link information flow to organizational form; conditions of existence of the collaborative model; design ICT for a specific collaborative enterprise.

**What it actually contains.**

- Organizations as **information processes** that reduce uncertainty enough to act (Benkler): markets via prices, firms via command, peer production via rich information about human capital.
- When peering is cheaper than market *and* hierarchy, peer production emerges; **information opportunity cost** as the hypothesis for why CBPP wins on matching people to tasks.
- ICT fields (logistics, demand patterns); traditional objections to the commons (motivation and organization).
- Information types (resources, procedural, communication, organizational knowledge, identity) and processes (transform, translate, transport, organize, store, qualify, consume).
- Feedback mechanisms named: current-sees, market pricing, managerial C2, reputation.
- Transparency and membranes (rights); dissemination; plan/predict; redundancy.
- Classifications of systems (subscription, filter, KM templates); coordination/liaison; work-process supports (bureaucracy, training, planning, overload).
- Information as product (services, patents, copyright).
- Costs: transaction costs, overload, quality, security, privacy.
- **Miscellania:** reading notes on *Coase’s Penguin* and a paper on TCT and IS (ICT can *increase* complexity/overload; TCT is a limited justification for IT). Closing definitions of p2p architecture vs distributed systems.

**Pedagogical form.** Graduate seminar packet: theory + annotated readings. Almost no “you will now design X for your venture.” Header still says jump to “Part 6: Innovation Strategy,” which is not what Part 6 is in Drive.

**Completion.** Overweight relative to Parts 6 and 8. The *feedback* in the title is underdeveloped compared with information taxonomy. No loop that produces a stigmergic environment (task board, public log, reputation surface) the students will actually use. ToDo: make a plan and propose how to move to Completion.

### Part 6 — Value proposition

**Intent (slides and website).** Value proposition and path to contributors, prosumers, consumers, users; incentive systems. Analysis and segmentation; collaboration and competition.

**What it actually contains.** Four short paragraphs:

- Identify stakeholders (close and distant), roles, contributions; sketch an incentive model.
- Entrepreneurs are not all game theorists; use instinct; **record assumptions**.
- Test assumptions with people who know games/networks, or learn the basics; honesty about how well the attractor works.
- Modify the model after verification.

**Pedagogical form.** A prompt, not a Part.

**Completion.** Unfinished. This is the hole in the middle of the program: after mapping value and writing a manifesto, the learner never designs **who is attracted, why they stay, and how benefits (plural) are experienced**. That is the load-bearing skill of collaborative entrepreneurship. ToDo: make a plan and propose how to move to Completion.

### Part 7 — Innovation strategy

- Types: business/org model; product/service; (implied) social, ecological, spatial, value — later in exercises.
- Sources: necessity, individuals, increasingly **networks of knowledge** / collective intelligence; incubators and clusters as industrial-policy response; open science / open source as further shift.

## Overview of the entire educational program (as designed and as delivered)



### Stated arc (11-part design)

The [program structure slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1wWQF_Uuyqxb4dE3CRf41cREDZ0WuDIo-vR9KECp20p8/edit) and the Sensorica education page describe a linear journey:

1. Enlarge possibles and fix notions
2. Map the value system (physics and law of flows)
3. Vision, mission, positioning
4. Resources
5. Information and feedback (and ICT)

6–7. Innovation strategy **and** value proposition / incentives (order disagrees by source)  
8. Operations  
9. Outreach (community vs network, onboarding, three pillars)  
10. Economic/business model and community model  
11. Create a collaborative venture (container: org + legal; REA intro)

Tagline: **do more with less.** Audience: any would-be entrepreneur (École des entrepreneurs style — no MBA prerequisite). First prototype: in-person at the Sensorica lab so early students **co-create the content** by their questions. Later: online materials, trainer badge.

### What the eight manuals actually deliver

A **front-loaded theory sequence** that is strongest at the beginning:

- Part 1 = paradigm shock and manifesto  
- Parts 2–5 = analytical language (flows, property, information)  
- Parts 6 and 8 = missing practice core  
- Part 7 = open-innovation argument, not yet a studio  
- Parts 9–11 = never written in this folder

So the program, as a student would experience it today, is: **learn to see like a Sensorican**, then **stop before you operate an open venture**. The original promise (“coached every step of the way to create a collaborative venture”) is not supported by the unfinished tail.

### Implicit pedagogy

- Teacher as explainer and contextualizer.  
- Documents as the primary medium (very long; “skim highlights”).  
- Occasional workshop (Part 2 mapping).  
- Little peer production *of the course itself* (except the coordination doc’s NRP log and recorded partner discussions).  
- One test student (Wael / Albaydar) whose venture leaked into the materials.

That is internally consistent with “traditional course format.” It is **externally inconsistent** with the object of teaching: permissionless contribution, stigmergy, long-tail production, and contribution accounting.

But we will think about evolving it away from being purely transactional and a one-to-many relation. There are many people involved in the thinking and doing of collaboartive entrepreneurship within and around the Sensorica network. We mostly want to turn this into a peer-learning environment, in some sense, this is coaborative entrepreneurship applied to education. Think about and propose a model for this course, one that offers sustainability for those who are involved in passing the knowledge, compatible with the collaboartive entrepreneurship model itself. ## Critics of this approach

ToDo: great points below, let's address them. 

These mix (a) what is known about **entrepreneurship education** in general, and (b) the contradiction between **this** content and **this** format.

### 1. The format contradicts the practice

Entrepreneurship education research (effectuation, lean startup, EntreComp, venture studios, peer-based incubators) has spent two decades moving away from “listen to a lecture, then write a business plan.” Even mainstream programs now use **doing under uncertainty**: customer interviews, MVPs, cohort critique, real resource constraints.

This program teaches **stigmergy, openness, and peer production** through a **broadcast**. Students do not join a permissionless context of work; they receive a packet. They do not log contributions to a shared venture; they contextualize theory. A learner can finish Part 1–5 fluent in holoptism and still never have published a call for participation.

### 2. Causation-first, effectuation-late (or never)

Sarasvathy’s effectuation: expert entrepreneurs start from means, affordable loss, partnerships, and contingency — not from a full vision then resources. This syllabus is **causal** in shape: possibles → map → vision → resources → information → (missing) operations. That matches MBA “strategy then execution.” It matches poorly with how open ventures actually start (seed value + attractor + whoever shows up).

Part 1 even tells people with a clear idea to **pretend they have no plan**. That is a useful unlearning move, but it is not followed by a means-driven studio. It is followed by more theory.

### 3. Information transmission is not skill formation

Knowing the Matrioshka numbers is not the same as being able to:

- write a public onboarding doc;  
- run a workparty;  
- keep a contribution log that others trust;  
- hold a red-flag without collapsing into a manager;  
- interface with a custodian or a hospital without capturing the network.

Those are **practices**. The manuals rarely specify observable performances, feedback, or “you are done when…”

### 4. Cognitive overload, no time architecture

Part 1 is a book chapter. Part 5 is an IS reading pack. There is no session length, no “one idea per meeting,” no spaced practice. Adult learners in entrepreneurship programs typically get **timeboxed sprints** and artifacts. Here the artifact is “understanding.” Overload produces the opposite of abundance mindset: students freeze.

### 5. The spine of a venture course is missing

Any entrepreneurship program, traditional or p2p, eventually has to answer: **who cares, why they engage, how work gets done, how money/liability is handled, how you talk to the outside.** That is Parts 6, 8, 9, 10, 11. Two are stubs; three do not exist. The program currently **cannot keep its own promise**.

### 6. Assessment is absent or contaminated

No rubric for a good value-system map, a viable manifesto, or a fair incentive sketch. Part 7’s exercises are filled with one venture. A later student cannot tell “example” from “the answer.”

### 7. Teacher-centered contextualization underuses the lab

The coordination doc wanted the first course **offline at the Sensorica lab** so students’ questions would shape content. That is the right instinct (studio, situated learning). The manuals still read as **content to deliver in a lab**, not as **the lab being the curriculum**.

### 8. Hybrid and traditional tools are mixed without a “when to use which”

Part 3 positioning, SMART goals, Amazon/Apple examples, business-model canvas (Part 7) sit next to nondominium and CAS. Without an explicit **adaptation rule** (what we keep, what we invert), students will default to the more familiar competitive-startup grammar — exactly what Part 1 tried to unsettle.

### 9. Complexity is named, not trained

Part 2 mentions complex dynamical systems; the wider Sensorica work (2025–2026) treats p2p as complexity-processing. The course never gives a **stress test**: 10× participation, founder exit, fork, funding drop. Dialogic tensions (openness vs quality, transparency vs privacy) are not practiced as design problems.

### 10. Phenomenology and lived fairness are missing

The later p2p-ness work insists that metrics must not replace lived agency, recognition, and trust. The manuals are almost silent on how a contributor *experiences* fairness. DAO-style failure (formally open, experientially alienating) is not a teaching object.

### 11. No cohort as network

Traditional accelerators at least force a **team**. This program addresses “an entrepreneur” in the singular. Collaborative entrepreneurship is **many entrepreneurs sharing risk**. There is no protocol for forming a temporary OVN *among the students* for the duration of the course. ToDo: great idea to implement. 

### 12. Maintenance and versioning of materials

Signalization boxes, “to be continued,” empty sources, wrong jump links, 2016–2017 market stats, and dual numbering show a living draft that was never closed. Teaching from it as-is will leak confusion.

## Suggestions — general approach

ToDo: great ideas, let's address tem all. 

These are recommendations for the **next** restructuring pass, not edits to Drive (per request: stop after this document).

1. **Make the course an open venture.** The program itself should have a public context of work, contribution logging, a benefit story (badge, reputation, commons license on student artifacts), and stigmergic tasks. Students learn collaborative entrepreneurship by **affiliating**, not only by listening.
2. **Flip the sequence toward effectuation + studio.** Each cycle: attractor → small public artifact → log → reflection with theory, rather than theory → later maybe a venture. Matrioshka-like constraints (cash cap, shared lab, timebox) beat another chapter.
3. **Define performances, not only topics.** Example performances: publish an onboarding page; map seed value with two other people; run a 2-hour workparty; propose a property regime for one resource; draft a license + benefit rule; hold a red-flag discussion.
4. **Finish the tail or shorten the head.** Either complete Parts 6, 8, 9–11 or cut the program to a coherent “paradigm + one studio loop.” Do not keep an 8-part list with two empty cores.
5. **Resolve numbering and the 8 vs 11 map** before rewriting, so website, slides, and manuals tell one story.
6. **Separate reference from session.** Keep long taxonomies (Part 4, Part 5) as **handbooks**. Sessions should be short, with a single artifact. “Skim highlights” is not a substitute for that split.
7. **Teach hybridity as a skill.** When to use a custodian, an exchange firm, a temporary NDA, a closed sprint — with capture tests (can the interface be revoked?).
8. **Put contribution accounting in the students’ hands in week 1,** even on paper. Do not postpone CAS to a tool they may never install.
9. **Peer critique over teacher contextualization.** Teacher as steward/facilitator of a field, not residual owner of meaning. Matches OVN roles.
10. **Update the complexity layer** using `what-is-collaborative-entrepreneurship.md`: allocation as engagement, dialogic tensions, path dependence, capital subordinate to commons. Do not add a “complexity lecture”; add **stress questions** to every studio.
11. **Audience honesty.** “Anyone” plus PhD-level Benkler/TCT readings will lose practitioners. Offer a **practice track** and a **theory appendix**, or two lengths.
12. **Remove one-student answers from the canon.** Cases in an appendix; blank exercises in the manual.
