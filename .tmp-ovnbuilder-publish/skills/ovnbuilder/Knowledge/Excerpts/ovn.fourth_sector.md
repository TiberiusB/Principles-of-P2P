# ovn.fourth_sector

The Fourth Sector is a set of principles, rules, and economic models that legitimize p2p, open, and collaborative socioeconomic practices. It is proposed in continuity with — and orthogonal to — the public, private, and solidarity (3rd / social) sectors. It is not a residual catch-all after those three.

Economy: p2p economics. Agents who start initiatives: collaborative entrepreneurs (vs bureaucrats / entrepreneurs / social entrepreneurs).

Distinctive axes (wiki characteristics):

- Support: organizations designed to function without needing the State for existence or the Market for subsistence (local or transnational; often non-registered); still may interface with grants, donations, or markets without collapsing identity.
- Property: primarily nondominium (assets no one owns exclusively; access under rules). Public → public property; private → private property; solidarity → shared property / commons (often state-protected).
- Value: multi-value / contribution-linked p2p value (not only public, social, or market value).
- Incentives: collaborative entrepreneurs build multi-value incentive webs that tend to rely less on money alone.
- Capabilities: non-bureaucratic; stigmergy and self-organization; suited to complexity.

Vs 3rd sector: social-economy legal forms and governance/economic models do not fully map to open network / p2p practice. Naming the 4th sector supports identity and delineation (property, governance, organizational architecture) and resists capture by bureaucratic or centralized boxes.

History (illustrative): term proposed by Sensorica in May 2019 in the Fourth Sector rationale (NOICE / Quebec collaborative-economy legal-framework effort). Not a claim of enacted national sector recognition.
